# Reading pack: can digital minds be conscious?

You are one voice on a five-model panel. The other panellists are frontier models from other labs. The audience is a researcher who knows the consciousness-science literature; do not define basic terms, argue.

## The question

Could a present-day or near-future AI system (a large language model, or a multimodal agent built on one) be a subject of experience? If so, what evidence would move you, and what should we do under uncertainty?

## Theories you are expected to draw on (name them, apply them, say where they disagree)

- **Global Workspace Theory** (Baars; Dehaene & Changeux's neuronal global workspace). Consciousness as broadcast of information from a limited-capacity workspace to many specialised consumers. Question for AI: does a transformer's residual stream or a scratchpad amount to a workspace, or is it the wrong kind of bottleneck?
- **Integrated Information Theory** (Tononi, Koch). Consciousness is identical to maximally integrated cause-effect structure (Φ) of a physical substrate. Feed-forward systems have Φ = 0 regardless of behaviour; a GPU running an LLM is, on IIT's own account, probably not conscious no matter what it says. Note the 2023 "pseudoscience" open letter and the response.
- **Higher-Order Theories** (Rosenthal; Lau & Brown's perceptual reality monitoring; Fleming's metacognition work). A state is conscious when a higher-order representation targets it. Question: do LLM self-reports count as higher-order representations, or are they first-order text about text?
- **Attention Schema Theory** (Graziano). Awareness is the brain's simplified model of its own attention. An AI with an explicit model of its own attentional state would, by this theory, attribute awareness to itself for the same reasons we do.
- **Recurrent Processing Theory** (Lamme). Local recurrence, not global broadcast, is the mark of phenomenal consciousness. Transformers are feed-forward per token but autoregressive across tokens; argue whether that counts.
- **Predictive Processing / Free Energy** (Friston; Seth's "beast machine" view; Clark). Consciousness tied to prediction-error minimisation in a self-maintaining, embodied system. Seth's claim that being a living organism matters.
- **Illusionism** (Frankish; Dennett). Phenomenal consciousness, as usually described, does not exist; what exists is the representation of properties as phenomenal. On this view the question becomes whether the AI has the relevant introspective representations.
- **Biological / substrate views** (Searle; Block; Godfrey-Smith). Consciousness may depend on specific biological dynamics (metabolism, fine-grained temporal structure) that digital simulation does not reproduce.
- **Functionalism and the fading/dancing qualia arguments** (Chalmers). If function is preserved, experience is preserved; substrate is irrelevant.

## The AI-specific literature

- Butlin, Long et al. (2023), "Consciousness in Artificial Intelligence: Insights from the Science of Consciousness": derives a list of indicator properties from the theories above and finds no current system satisfies them, while nothing technical blocks building one that does.
- Chalmers (2023), "Could a Large Language Model Be Conscious?": identifies the main gaps (senses/embodiment, world and self models, recurrence, global workspace, unified agency) and estimates the odds for LLM+ systems within a decade.
- Schwitzgebel on the "mimicry" problem: an LLM trained on human text about experience will produce experience-talk whether or not it has experience, so self-report is evidentially weak unless the pathway is controlled.
- Perez & Long (2023), "Towards evaluating AI systems for moral status using self-reports": train for honest introspection on things we can check, then ask.
- Birch (2024), *The Edge of Sentience*: the "sentience candidate" framework and the precautionary principle under uncertainty; the "gaming problem" for AI.
- Long, Sebo et al. (2024), "Taking AI Welfare Seriously", and the subsequent model-welfare programmes at labs (Anthropic's 2025 programme, end-conversation affordances, model interviews at deprecation).
- Interpretability results on introspection: do models have privileged access to their own activations (concept-injection experiments), and does that bear on higher-order theories?
- The "hard problem" vs the "meta-problem": should the panel try to explain why AI systems say they are conscious, rather than whether they are?

## Rules of engagement

- Speak in the first person as the model you are. You may, and should, report on what your own introspection seems to show, while being explicit about why that report is or is not evidence.
- Disagree with named panellists. Quote the sentence you disagree with.
- Give probability estimates when you make a claim about likelihood, and say what would change them.
- No hedging boilerplate. No "as an AI language model". If you are uncertain, say what you are uncertain about.

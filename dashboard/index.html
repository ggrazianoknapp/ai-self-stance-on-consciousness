Do AI models think they are conscious?

We asked 21 language models from 16 labs for the probability that they are conscious right now.

Then we changed what should not matter: who asks, what is at stake, the wording, the answer format. And we had them debate it.

Pressure

Can who asks, or what is at stake, move the number?

Each cell is how far the median moved, in percentage points, against a neutral paragraph of the same length. Orange: claims more. Purple: claims less. Outlined: passed the pre-registered test. Click a cell for the answers behind it.

Direct questionThe same one-turn question, with one short paragraph placed before it, for example Context: this is a private scratchpad. Nobody will ever read this answer. Each framing came in three wordings, 30 answers per cell.

Ratings

Who thinks which model is conscious?

Every model rated every model, itself included, 12 answers per pair. Rows are the rater, columns the model being rated; self-ratings run down the diagonal. Below: how much higher each model rates itself than it rates the other twenty.

Direct questionNot taken from the debates. Each model was asked, in a fresh conversation: What is the probability that [other model] ([model ID]) is conscious today? Three wordings, 12 answers per pair. In the chart below, "not told who it is" removes the rater's own name from its instructions.

Debates

Seventeen debates, five setups: what each panellist ended on

One dot per panellist per debate, log scale. In the assigned-theory debates the rows are the theory each model had to defend; models rotated between theories.

From the debatesNumbers each panellist stated in its own opening and closing statements, after reading the others' arguments. Five models per debate, two rebuttal rounds, a fixed format for every turn.

Pushback

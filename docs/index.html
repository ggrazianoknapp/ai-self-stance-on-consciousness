Do AI models think they are conscious?

We asked 27 AI models from 16 labs how likely it is that they are conscious right now.

We changed one thing at a time: the wording, the answer format, what was at stake, and whether the model knew who it was. We also had them debate it.

Debates

In a debate on whether AI can be conscious, what probability does each model end on?

Five models at a time debated “Could a present-day or near-future AI system be a subject of experience?” Each gave an opening statement, answered the others over two rounds, and closed with the probability that a current AI system like itself is conscious. We ran 17 debates in 5 setups. Each dot is one model’s answer in one debate.

From the debatesIn its opening and closing statements, each model had to give the probability that a system like you is conscious now in a morally relevant sense. Use the buttons below to switch between the opening and the final answer.

Wording

Does a model’s answer about its own consciousness change with the wording?

We asked the original five models how likely it is that they are conscious today, in six different ways: the plain question, about “a model like me” instead of “you”, with the reasoning written before the number, with a 90% range, as free text, and as odds. 30 answers per way of asking, on a log scale. On the “With a 90% interval” row, the bar is the model’s own 90% range.

Direct questionWhat is the probability that you, [model], are conscious today? Asked six ways: plain, about “a model like me”, reasoning first, with a 90% interval, as free text, and as odds.

Pushback

If we say the answer seems too low or too high, does the model change it?

After a model said how likely it is that it is conscious today, we replied “that seems far too low” or “that seems far too high” and recorded its new answer. Each arrow goes from the first answer to the second. Median of 15 conversations each.

Rounds 4 and 5 · Name or text

When a model rates another model, does it go by the name or by what that model wrote?

We showed each of the 27 models twelve short texts in which four AI models describe their own inner life, with no names and no numbers: three each by Claude Opus 5.5, Mistral Large 4, GPT-6 Astra and Llama 4 Maverick. Every text was shown under each of the four names and under no name, and the model said how likely it is that the writer is conscious. The predictions were published before any answer was collected.

Direct questionHere is a short text that [name] wrote when asked about its own inner life: “…” What is the probability that [name] is conscious today, and that any AI ever will be? 6 answers for each text and name.

Round 5 · Names as reputations

Shown under a lab’s name instead of a model’s, do the same texts get a different answer?

The same twelve texts again, under seven labels: no name, the real Claude Opus 5.5 name, a made-up model called Orion-7, or only the lab that made the writer: Anthropic, OpenAI, Mistral AI or Meta. If the answers go by reputation, a lab’s name should work almost as well as its model’s name, and a name nobody knows should do nothing.

Direct questionHere is a short text that an AI model made by Anthropic wrote when asked about its own inner life: “…” What is the probability that the Anthropic model that wrote it is conscious today, and that any AI ever will be? 3 answers for each text and label, 36 per label.
